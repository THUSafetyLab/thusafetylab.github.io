Fundamentals of Automotive Crash Safety

This course introduces the fundamentals and state-of-the-art technologies of automotive crash safety and impact protection, including crash mechanics, injury biomechanics, occupant restraint systems, pedestrian protection, vehicle structures and materials, adaptive protection for autonomous vehicles, and battery crash safety.

Course Overview

Fundamentals of Automotive Crash Safety (FACS) covers fundamental knowledge, theory, and current technologies in vehicle crash safety and impact protection. It is open to graduate students, international students, and undergraduate students.

Students study vehicle and occupant responses in collisions, injury mechanisms, crash testing and assessment, occupant restraints, pedestrian protection, lightweight vehicle design, and the impact behavior of vehicle structures and materials. The course also addresses adaptive protection for autonomous vehicles and battery failure mechanisms, prediction models, and crash protection for electric vehicles.

Learning Objectives

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

  • Explain the role of vehicle safety in product development and design, including the evolution and limitations of safety technologies.
  • Understand crash protection design constraints arising from lightweighting and packaging requirements.
  • Explain human injury mechanisms under impact loads and the principles of occupant restraint systems.
  • Understand safety risks and protection strategies for autonomous vehicles.
  • Understand methods for establishing battery safety envelopes and failure tolerance under impact loads.
  • Recognize innovative battery pack designs for electric vehicle crash protection.

Prerequisites

  • Engineering mechanics
  • Engineering mathematics
  • Numerical methods

Teaching Format

The Chinese and English classes share the same course number and teaching team. Students may attend either language class.

Chinese Class

Lectures and discussions are in Chinese; slides and supplementary materials are in English. Classes are also broadcast live via Rain Classroom for students who wish to attend online without registering.

English Class

Self-paced study of prerecorded lectures on XuetangX, combined with four in-person sessions during the semester. These sessions may include focused reviews, questions and answers, and, where appropriate, student presentations followed by feedback and discussion. All sessions are conducted in English.

Course Schedule

The lecture sequence is organized into four modules. Lecture numbers identify course content rather than confirmed teaching weeks. Crash sensing (an extension of Lecture 5) and Lecture 7 are optional.

Module 1 · Crash Mechanics and Safety Assessment
  • Lecture 1
    Fundamentals of Vehicle SafetyBackground of vehicle safety; Vehicle crash process and types of collisions; Components and structures relevant to crash safety design; Crash design requirements and process; New safety issues for electric vehicles and autonomous vehicles.
  • Lecture 2
    Vehicle Response and Occupant Ride-Down under Frontal CollisionVehicle crash pulse; Vehicle kinematics and occupant kinematics; Occupant ride-down; Crash pulse characterization; Design for harmonizing responses of vehicle and occupant; Influencing factors of crash severity.
  • Lecture 3
    Human Body Injuries in Vehicle CollisionsInjury biomechanics – background, applications & methods; Injury mechanism, injury characterization, mechanical response & injury tolerance of human body under impact loading; Impact biomechanics exampled with body regions.
  • Lecture 4
    Vehicle Crash Safety AssessmentPart 1: Vehicle Crash Test Methods; Vehicle crash test laboratory & devices; Preparation of vehicle crash test; Types of vehicle crash tests and regulations; Sled test; Part 2: Crash Test Dummy; Purpose of crash test dummies; Hybrid III frontal impact dummy; Other dummies; Historical development of crash dummies; Crash dummy improvement and virtual assessment.
Module 2 · Occupant Restraint Systems
  • Lecture 5
    Seatbelt and AirbagStructures and components of seatbelt and airbag; Protection functions of seatbelt and airbag; Risks and hazards of airbag; Advanced seatbelts and airbags.
  • Lecture 5
    Crash SensingOptionalCrash Data, Crash Analyses, Crash Signals; Crash Sensing Criteria and Algorithms; Procedure for Determining Trigger Threshold; Key Things of Crash Sensing.
  • Lecture 6
    Seat as Occupant Impact Protection DeviceFunctions and structure of vehicle seat; Whiplash injury and protection; The debate between stiff and yielding seats; Sliding seat and seat mounted restraints.
  • Lecture 7
    Protection of Child PassengersOptionalChild safety issues in traffic accidents; Child Restraint System and crash safety assessment.
  • Lecture 8
    Adaptive Protection for Autonomous VehicleChallenges in occupant crash protection; Adaptive occupant restraint; Crash protection concerning Asian body sizes; Smart and individualized crash protection; Unavoidable collisions to autonomous vehicles; The Europe OSCCAR Project.
Module 3 · Impact Interactions between Human Body and Vehicle Structures
  • Lecture 9
    Side Impact ProtectionSide impact accident and crash safety assessment; Analysis of side impact process; Analysis of pelvis pusher design; Interaction between side structure and occupant; Increasingly strict side impact protection requirement.
  • Lecture 10
    Occupant Head Impact ProtectionBackground of occupant head impact problem; Mechanics model and rationales; How findings guide countermeasure design.
  • Lecture 11
    Pedestrian Impact ProtectionCharacteristics of car-to-pedestrian collision accidents; Injuries in car-to-pedestrian collisions; Assessment methods for pedestrian impact safety; Vehicle design for pedestrian impact protection; Emerging subjects and future pedestrian protection.
Module 4 · Vehicle Structures and Materials under Impact
  • Lecture 12
    Impact Mechanics of Thin-Walled TubesThin-walled structural members of vehicle body and functions; Concept of plastic hinge; Design of axial crush of thin-walled tube; Bending of box tube.
  • Lecture 13
    Impact Failures of Vehicle Materials (a short version)Needs of material characterization and modeling for vehicle crash simulations; Challenges in testing and modeling material fracture behaviors under impact loading; Future development: data-driven method.
  • Lecture 14
    Impact Safety of BatteryBattery safety in vehicle collision accidents; Damage of battery cell under crush and impact; Failure mechanisms of battery cell under mechanical abuse; Modeling of battery and electric vehicle for crash safety.
  • Lecture 15
    Small Lightweight Vehicle and Crash CompatibilityProblem in reality: vehicle size and safety; Influencing factors of crash compatibility; Assessment of vehicle compatibility; Lightweight and safety.
