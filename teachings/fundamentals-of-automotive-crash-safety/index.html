Fundamentals of Automotive Crash Safety

This course introduces the scientific foundations and advanced technologies of automotive crash safety and impact protection. Topics include crash mechanics, injury biomechanics, occupant restraint systems, pedestrian protection, the impact behavior of vehicle structures and materials, adaptive protection for autonomous vehicles, and battery crash safety.

Course Overview

Fundamentals of Automotive Crash Safety (FACS) introduces the scientific principles and engineering methods used to understand crashes and design effective protection. The course welcomes undergraduate and graduate students, including international students.

Students study vehicle and occupant responses in collisions, injury mechanisms, crash testing and assessment, occupant restraints, pedestrian protection, lightweight vehicle design, and the impact behavior of vehicle structures and materials. The course also addresses adaptive protection for autonomous vehicles and battery failure mechanisms, prediction models, and crash protection for electric vehicles.

Learning Objectives

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

  • Explain the role of vehicle safety in product development and design, including the evolution and limitations of safety technologies.
  • Explain how lightweighting and vehicle packaging requirements constrain crash protection design.
  • Explain human injury mechanisms under impact loads and the principles of occupant restraint systems.
  • Describe safety risks and protection strategies for autonomous vehicles.
  • Describe methods for establishing battery safety envelopes and failure tolerance under impact loading.
  • Identify innovative battery pack designs that improve electric vehicle crash protection.

Prerequisites

  • Engineering mechanics
  • Engineering mathematics
  • Numerical methods

Teaching Format

The course offers separate Chinese- and English-language sections with the same course number and teaching team. Students may attend either section.

Chinese-Language Section

Lectures and discussions are conducted in Chinese, with slides and supplementary materials in English. Sessions are also streamed live via Rain Classroom for students who wish to audit the course online without enrolling.

English-Language Section

Students study prerecorded lectures on XuetangX at their own pace and attend four in-person sessions during the semester. These sessions may include reviews of key topics, questions and answers, and, where appropriate, student presentations with feedback and discussion. All sessions are conducted in English.

Course Schedule

The syllabus is organized into four modules. Lecture numbers indicate the sequence of topics and do not correspond to specific teaching weeks. Crash Sensing (an extension of Lecture 5) and Lecture 7 are optional.

Module 1 · Crash Mechanics and Safety Assessment
  • Lecture 1
    Fundamentals of Vehicle SafetyAn introduction to vehicle safety; crash sequences and collision types; components and structures involved in crash protection; crash safety design requirements and processes; emerging safety challenges for electric and autonomous vehicles.
  • Lecture 2
    Vehicle Response and Occupant Ride-Down in Frontal CrashesVehicle crash pulses and their characterization; vehicle and occupant kinematics; occupant ride-down; design strategies for coordinating vehicle and occupant responses; factors affecting crash severity.
  • Lecture 3
    Injury Biomechanics in Vehicle CrashesFoundations, applications, and methods of injury biomechanics; injury mechanisms and characterization; human mechanical response and injury tolerance under impact loading; examples organized by body region.
  • Lecture 4
    Vehicle Crash Safety AssessmentPart 1: Crash test methods, facilities, equipment, and preparation; test types and applicable regulations; sled testing. Part 2: The purpose and development of crash test dummies, including the Hybrid III frontal impact dummy and other dummy designs; improvements to dummies and their evaluation through simulation.
Module 2 · Occupant Restraint Systems
  • Lecture 5
    Seat Belts and AirbagsDesign and components of seat belts and airbags; principles of occupant protection; risks associated with airbag deployment; advanced seat belt and airbag technologies.
  • Lecture 5
    Crash SensingOptionalCrash data, analysis, and signals; crash sensing criteria and algorithms; procedures for determining deployment thresholds; key considerations in crash sensing.
  • Lecture 6
    Vehicle Seats and Occupant Crash ProtectionFunctions and structure of vehicle seats; whiplash injury and prevention; trade-offs between stiff and yielding seat designs; sliding seats and seat-mounted restraints.
  • Lecture 7
    Child Occupant ProtectionOptionalChild occupant safety in road traffic crashes; child restraint systems and assessment of their crash protection performance.
  • Lecture 8
    Adaptive Occupant Protection for Autonomous VehiclesChallenges in occupant crash protection; adaptive restraint systems; body size variation in Asian populations and its implications for protection; intelligent and personalized crash protection; unavoidable collisions involving autonomous vehicles; the European OSCCAR project.
Module 3 · Impact Interactions between the Human Body and Vehicle Structures
  • Lecture 9
    Side Impact ProtectionSide impact crashes and safety assessment; analysis of side impact dynamics and pelvis pusher design; interactions between the vehicle side structure and the occupant; increasingly stringent requirements for side impact protection.
  • Lecture 10
    Occupant Head Impact ProtectionOccupant head impact risks; mechanical models and their underlying principles; application of research findings to the design of protective measures.
  • Lecture 11
    Pedestrian Impact ProtectionCharacteristics of vehicle–pedestrian collisions and resulting injuries; methods for assessing pedestrian protection; vehicle design to reduce pedestrian injuries; emerging research and future directions in pedestrian safety.
Module 4 · Vehicle Structures and Materials under Impact
  • Lecture 12
    Impact Mechanics of Thin-Walled TubesThin-walled vehicle body structures and their functions; the plastic hinge concept; design of thin-walled tubes for axial crushing; bending of box-section tubes.
  • Lecture 13
    Material Failure under Impact Loading (Overview)Material characterization and modeling requirements for vehicle crash simulation; challenges in testing and modeling fracture under impact loading; emerging data-driven methods.
  • Lecture 14
    Battery Crash SafetyBattery safety in vehicle crashes; cell damage under crush and impact loading; cell failure mechanisms under mechanical abuse; battery and electric vehicle modeling for crash safety assessment.
  • Lecture 15
    Small, Lightweight Vehicles and Crash CompatibilityThe relationship between vehicle size and crash safety; factors affecting crash compatibility; methods for assessing compatibility; balancing weight reduction with crash protection.
